Video presence

A proposal for the next iteration: licensed video calls with a talent's approved likeness and voice, the member-side look mixer, the vendor rig ingest gate, and the Talent Care agent that keeps every persona current.

Nineteen screens for your approval. Nothing here is built yet. Every decision you made in our exchange is carried through, and the three places I have overruled myself are marked on the page for you to reverse.

How this sits on what exists

The v0.4 prototype already carries the rights model, the precedence engine, the record, the training studio and the crisis experience. Video is not a new product bolted on the side — it is the same rights chain with a face attached, and it inherits every guard.

What video inherits unchanged

  • The real-person guard. No real, identifiable person becomes callable in the prototype. The demonstrable personas stay invented; real names stay business facts in the pipeline.
  • The precedence engine. Platform policy, then talent boundaries, then member preferences — narrowing only. A look, a setting or a topic on a call passes the same function a text reply does.
  • The record. Identifiers, versions and statuses. A call writes that it happened, its duration and which rig version spoke. It never stores a frame, a word, or a still.
  • The crisis experience. Extended to video rather than duplicated — see screen 9, which is the screen I most want you to look at.
  • The attachment safeguards. No guilt copy, no upgrade prompt in a room, the sixty-minute notice, sensitive categories never written to memory.

What video adds

  • W- requirements — the call itself: entry, disclosure, controls, captions, minutes, ending, callback.
  • N- requirements — name and likeness: the vendor rig, its ingest gate, the member-side mixer, and the permanent on-screen disclosure.
  • H- requirements — talent health: the freshness clock, the escalation ladder, the Talent Care agent, and the hold.
  • A third boundary band for suggestive-not-explicit, calibrated per talent rather than globally.
  • A script library with third-party clearance, which is how "insider info" becomes safe to ship.

What it removes

  • The relationship-depth percentage, per your anti-gamification answer.
  • Six loose nouns, replaced by four precise ones.

When the call ends

The moment most products use to ask for a rating, sell an upgrade, or start a streak. This one does none of those, and the restraint is the design.

1
What it remembered is shown immediately and is editable on the spot — the moment a member most wants to correct something is the moment they just said it.
2
Sensitive categories never appear here, because they were never written. Crisis, health, abuse, finances and immigration status are excluded from memory on every path, video included.
3
No star rating and no "how did that go?" Quality signal for a licensed likeness belongs in the studio's aggregate reporting, not extracted from the member at their softest moment.
4
No upgrade prompt, even though this is commercially the best place for one. The rule is no plan prompt inside a room, and the end of a call is inside the room.
5
Minutes are reported, once, without comment. No "top up now" call to action here; that lives on screen 11 where the member goes deliberately.

Your idea, and the most novel thing in this proposal: an agent pointed at the talent rather than the public. It nudges, escalates, de-escalates, and recommends the hold.

What this proposal does not build

The same discipline as every iteration: the exclusions are written down before the work starts, so nobody has to guess later whether something was forgotten or refused.

Not built, by rule

  • No synthesised likeness of any real person. The prototype renders an honest placeholder and labels it. The rig ingest screen demonstrates the gate; it never produces a face.
  • No real video, audio or render pipeline. Calls are simulated states on the seeded clock, exactly as voice already is. There is no media file anywhere in the build.
  • No real-time inference, speech or identity vendor. Every one stays a mock adapter with a documented contract and a named production home.
  • No payment rail. Minutes and top-ups are simulated; no card form exists on any screen.
  • No live human on any call, per your answer. There is no scheduling or payout flow for human time, and no path where a real person joins.
  • No politicians on the roster.
  • No explicit content. Blocked at the platform layer, which no grant reopens.

Deferred, and labelled as such on screen

  • Group calls. The cohort on one call together is the obvious next thing and is a placeholder only.
  • Real screen-recording prevention. Cannot be solved on a general-purpose device; detected where possible, named as a limit in the agreement.
  • A real suggestive/explicit classifier. The prototype uses a keyword fixture and says so. Production needs a trained model with per-talent calibration.
  • A real third-party entity resolver for the script detector. Fixture in v0.5.
  • Render fidelity scoring. No honest metric exists until real rigs arrive.

Carried in unchanged

  • The rights guards, the precedence engine, the record and its tombstones, the crisis experience, the attachment safeguards, the adapter registry, and the determinism rules.
